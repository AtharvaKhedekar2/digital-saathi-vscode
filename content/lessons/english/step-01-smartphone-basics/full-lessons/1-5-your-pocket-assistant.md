## SECTION 1 · STORY

### 🎭 Story: "The Missed Tablet and the Late Bill"

👤 **Meet Dinesh Nair.** He is 74 and lives in Kochi. He retired from the railways, and he has a quiet morning routine: tea, newspaper, and a small white tablet for his blood pressure at 9 am.

**The situation.** Dinesh Nair has a lot on his mind these days. His granddaughter is getting married, and the house is full of guests. His mind is busy, and his routine has become a little muddled.

**The problem.** Twice this week, he forgot his 9 am tablet. Then a letter came: his **electricity bill** was overdue, and he had to pay a **late fee**. He felt upset with himself. *"My memory is not what it used to be,"* he said sadly.

**How the phone helps.** His son, Anil, visits that evening and says: *"Appa, you do not need to remember everything. Just tell your phone."* He picks up the phone and says, clearly: **"Hey Google, remind me to take my medicine every day at 9 am."** The phone answers and confirms. Then Anil says: **"Remind me to pay the electricity bill on the 5th."** Again the phone confirms.

**The happy ending.** The next morning at 9 o'clock, the phone gives a gentle alert. Dinesh Nair smiles, takes his tablet, and taps **Done**. On the 5th, the phone reminds him to pay the bill, on time, with no late fee. *"My phone remembers things so I can enjoy my day,"* he says. He starts using it for the family's birthdays too.

## SECTION 2 · WHAT IF?

### ❓ What if typing is difficult or your hands are busy?

**Meet Radha Krishnan**, 70, from Madurai. She has stiff fingers from arthritis, and typing on the small screen is painful. Today, her hands are covered in flour while she is making chapatis. She suddenly remembers that she has to book her blood test.

She does not wash her hands or find her phone's keyboard. She simply says: **"Hey Google, remind me to book my blood test tomorrow at 10 am."** The reminder is set in seconds. Not a single letter was typed.

**A small safety tip.** Reminders help you remember, but they do not replace your doctor's advice. Take your medicines exactly as your doctor has told you. If you are not sure, ask your doctor or chemist.

**The lesson:** Speaking is the easiest way to use a phone, especially when typing is hard.

## SECTION 3 · HOW TO SET IT UP

### 🛠️ How To Set It Up

*(Android phone. "Hey Google" must be turned on. If your phone does not answer, ask a family member to switch on "Hey Google" once.)*

**Step 1.** Pick up your phone and say clearly: **"Hey Google"**. Wait for the phone to listen.

**Step 2.** Say your reminder in full, for example: **"Remind me to take my medicine every day at 9 am."**

**Step 3.** Try another one: **"Remind me to pay the electricity bill on the 5th."**

**Step 4.** The phone confirms what it has set. When the time comes, your phone will remind you. Tap **Done** (or “Mark as done”) when you have finished.

💡 **Tips:**
- Speak slowly and clearly, in a quiet place.
- Say **what** and **when**, for example: "remind me to ... at ..." or "... on the 5th".
- If the phone did not understand, just say it again.

## SECTION 4 · INTERACTIVE ACTIVITY

### 🎮 Interactive Activity: "Kusum's Two Reminders"

**Scenario.** Kusum, 69, lives in Lucknow. She wants her phone to remind her about her sugar tablet every evening at 8 pm. She also wants a reminder to pay her gas bill on the 10th of every month.

**Question.** Which sentences should Kusum say to her phone? *(Tick all that are correct.)*

- ☐ "Hey Google, remind me to take my sugar tablet every day at 8 pm."
- ☐ "Hey Google, remind me to pay the gas bill on the 10th."
- ☐ "Hey Google, forget my bills."
- ☐ Say nothing and hope she will remember
- ☐ Tell a stranger on the bus to remember for her

**✅ Correct answer:** The first two:
- "Hey Google, remind me to take my sugar tablet every day at 8 pm."
- "Hey Google, remind me to pay the gas bill on the 10th."

*Why the others are wrong:* Telling the phone to forget will not help, and hoping to remember is exactly the problem. A stranger cannot be responsible for your medicine.

## SECTION 8 · QUIZ

### 📝 Quiz: Your Pocket Assistant

**5 Multiple-Choice Questions**

**MCQ 1.** What do you say first to wake up the voice helper on many Android phones?
- A. "Goodbye phone"
- B. "Hey Google"
- C. "Switch off"
- D. "Delete everything"

✅ **Answer: B.**

**MCQ 2.** Which of these is a good reminder sentence?
- A. "Remind me to take my medicine every day at 9 am."
- B. "Medicine."
- C. "Hello hello."
- D. "9."

✅ **Answer: A.** Say **what** and **when**.

**MCQ 3.** How do you set a bill reminder by voice?
- A. "Electricity."
- B. "Bill, bill, bill."
- C. "Never pay."
- D. "Remind me to pay the electricity bill on the 5th."

✅ **Answer: D.**

**MCQ 4.** What should you do if the phone did not understand you?
- A. Throw the phone away
- B. Shout at the phone
- C. Say it again, slowly and clearly
- D. Give up on voice reminders

✅ **Answer: C.**

**MCQ 5.** When the reminder rings, what do you tap after you have taken your medicine?
- A. Delete all
- B. Switch off the phone
- C. Share
- D. Done

✅ **Answer: D.**

**3 True / False Questions**

**T/F 1.** You need to type the reminder letter by letter.
✅ **False.** You can set it just by speaking.

**T/F 2.** A phone reminder replaces your doctor's advice.
✅ **False.** It only reminds you. Take medicines as your doctor told you.

**T/F 3.** You can set reminders for bills as well as medicines.
✅ **True.**

**1 Scenario-Based Question**

**Scenario.** Hema, 68, has her hands full of wet laundry. She suddenly remembers she must pay her phone bill on the 12th. What is the best thing to do?
- A. Wash and dry her hands, find her glasses, and type a long note
- B. Say: "Hey Google, remind me to pay my phone bill on the 12th."
- C. Forget about it
- D. Ask a stranger to remember it

✅ **Answer: B.** It takes a few seconds, with no typing.
